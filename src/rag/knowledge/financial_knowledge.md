# Consumer Protection & Chargeback Rights
---
id: chargeback_rights_overview
tags: chargeback, dispute, refund, credit card, consumer protection
---
Under the Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA), you have the right to dispute charges on your credit card for:
- Unauthorized charges (fraud)
- Charges for goods or services not delivered
- Charges for goods or services that were significantly different from what was described
- Billing errors (wrong amount, duplicate charges)
You must file the dispute within 60 days of the statement date on which the charge appeared. The card issuer must acknowledge your dispute within 30 days and resolve it within two billing cycles (but no more than 90 days). During the investigation, you are not required to pay the disputed amount. If the issuer finds in your favor, the charge and any related finance charges are removed.

---
id: chargeback_debit_vs_credit
tags: chargeback, debit card, credit card, dispute, liability
---
Debit card disputes are governed by the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA/Regulation E), NOT the FCBA. Your liability depends on how quickly you report:
- Within 2 business days: max $50 liability
- Between 2 and 60 days: max $500 liability  
- After 60 days: potentially unlimited liability
This is a critical reason to use credit cards instead of debit cards for purchases. Credit cards cap your liability at $0 for unauthorized charges under most issuers' zero-liability policies. With debit, the money leaves your checking account immediately and you must fight to get it back.

---
id: chargeback_valid_reasons
tags: chargeback, dispute, valid reasons, fraud
---
Valid chargeback reasons that banks typically approve:
1. Item never received (merchant failed to deliver)
2. Item significantly not as described (wrong item, defective, counterfeit)
3. Duplicate charge (charged twice for same purchase)
4. Subscription you already cancelled still billing
5. Unauthorized transaction (your card was stolen/compromised)
6. Merchant refused a valid refund for a returned item
7. Services not rendered (paid for a service that was never performed)

Invalid reasons that will likely be denied:
1. Buyer's remorse (you changed your mind)
2. You don't recognize the charge but it was actually yours
3. A family member used your card with implied permission
4. You received the item but just don't like it (return it instead)

---
Major retailer price match and price adjustment policies (2024-2025):
- **Best Buy**: 15-day price match guarantee on identical items. Matches Amazon, Walmart, and local competitors. Does NOT match marketplace sellers.
- **Target**: 14-day price adjustment on purchases. Also matches select online competitors at time of purchase.
- **Costco**: 30-day price adjustment on most items. Exceptions: electronics have a 90-day return window.
- **Apple**: 14-day price adjustment on Apple Store purchases.
- **Amazon**: No official price match policy, but customer service sometimes issues courtesy credits on recent purchases where the price dropped.
- **Walmart**: No longer price-matches competitors as of 2020. Will match Walmart.com prices in-store.
- **Nordstrom**: Will price-match if the item goes on sale within 14 days of purchase.

To request a price adjustment: contact customer service with your order number and proof of the lower price. Many retailers process this as a partial refund to your original payment method.

---
id: return_policy_tips
tags: return, refund, receipt, policy, consumer rights
---
Return tips that most people don't know:
1. **No receipt?** Most major retailers can look up purchases by the credit/debit card used. Target, Walmart, Home Depot, and Best Buy all support receipt-less returns via card lookup.
2. **Extended holiday returns**: Most retailers extend return windows for purchases made Nov-Dec. Items bought in November can typically be returned through late January.
3. **Credit card return protection**: Many premium credit cards (Amex Platinum, Chase Sapphire) offer "Return Protection" — if a retailer refuses your return within 90 days, the card issuer will refund you up to $300-$500 per item.
4. **Defective after warranty?** Contact the manufacturer directly. Many will replace items outside the official warranty as a goodwill gesture if you're polite and persistent.
5. **Credit card extended warranty**: Cards like Citi Double Cash, Chase Freedom, and Amex automatically extend manufacturer warranties by 1-2 years at no cost.

# Tax Knowledge
---
id: tax_standard_vs_itemized
tags: tax, deduction, standard deduction, itemized, filing
---
For tax year 2025, the standard deduction is:
- Single / Married Filing Separately: $15,000
- Married Filing Jointly: $30,000
- Head of Household: $22,500

You should itemize ONLY if your total itemizable deductions exceed your standard deduction. Common itemizable deductions include:
- State and local taxes (SALT) — capped at $10,000
- Mortgage interest on loans up to $750,000
- Charitable contributions (cash up to 60% of AGI, appreciated stock up to 30%)
- Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI
- Casualty and theft losses from federally declared disasters

For most renters with no mortgage, the standard deduction is almost always better.

---
id: tax_deductible_common
tags: tax, deduction, write-off, self-employed, freelance, 1099
---
Commonly missed tax deductions for self-employed / 1099 workers:
1. **Home office deduction**: Simplified method = $5/sq ft, up to 300 sq ft ($1,500 max). Regular method = actual expenses prorated by office percentage of home.
2. **Internet and phone**: Deductible proportionally to business use. If 50% of your internet usage is for work, deduct 50% of the bill.
3. **Health insurance premiums**: 100% deductible for self-employed if you're not eligible for employer coverage.
4. **Self-employment tax deduction**: You can deduct 50% of your SE tax from gross income.
5. **Retirement contributions**: SEP-IRA allows up to 25% of net SE income (max $69,000 for 2024). Solo 401(k) allows up to $23,000 employee + 25% employer.
6. **Business meals**: 50% deductible if directly related to business (must document who, what, where, business purpose).
7. **Mileage**: 67 cents/mile for 2024 business driving. Keep a mileage log.
8. **Software and subscriptions**: Fully deductible if used for business (Adobe, GitHub, cloud hosting, etc.).
9. **Education**: Courses and certifications that maintain or improve skills in your current field are deductible.
10. **Bank fees and interest**: Business account fees, credit card processing fees, and business loan interest.

---
id: tax_estimated_payments
tags: tax, estimated payments, quarterly, penalty, self-employed
---
If you owe more than $1,000 in taxes at filing time, the IRS charges an underpayment penalty. To avoid this, you must make quarterly estimated tax payments:
- Q1: Due April 15
- Q2: Due June 15
- Q3: Due September 15
- Q4: Due January 15

Safe harbor rule: You avoid the penalty if you pay at least:
- 100% of last year's tax liability (110% if AGI > $150,000), OR
- 90% of the current year's tax liability

Use IRS Form 1040-ES to calculate and pay. Payments can be made at irs.gov/payments via Direct Pay (free) or credit card (fees apply).

---
id: tax_capital_gains
tags: tax, capital gains, stocks, investments, crypto
---
Capital gains tax rates for 2025:
- **Short-term** (held < 1 year): Taxed as ordinary income (10%-37%)
- **Long-term** (held ≥ 1 year):
  - 0% if taxable income ≤ $47,025 (single) / $94,050 (married)
  - 15% if taxable income ≤ $518,900 (single) / $583,750 (married)
  - 20% above those thresholds

**Tax-loss harvesting**: You can sell losing investments to offset gains. Up to $3,000 of net losses can be deducted against ordinary income per year. Excess losses carry forward indefinitely. Be careful of the wash-sale rule: you cannot repurchase a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale.

**Crypto**: The IRS treats crypto as property. Every trade, swap, or spending of crypto is a taxable event. Receiving crypto as payment is ordinary income at fair market value at time of receipt.

# Credit Card Optimization
---
id: credit_card_interest_math
tags: credit card, interest, APR, minimum payment, debt
---
How credit card interest actually works:
Your APR is divided by 365 to get the Daily Periodic Rate (DPR). Interest accrues daily on your average daily balance.
Example: $5,000 balance at 24.99% APR
- DPR = 24.99% / 365 = 0.0685% per day
- Daily interest = $5,000 × 0.000685 = $3.42/day
- Monthly interest ≈ $104

If you only pay the minimum ($25 + interest = ~$129/month), it will take approximately 30 years to pay off and you will pay over $9,000 in interest on a $5,000 balance.

The single most impactful financial move for someone carrying credit card debt: pay MORE than the minimum. Even $50 extra per month dramatically changes the payoff timeline.

---
id: credit_card_grace_period
tags: credit card, grace period, interest, payment, billing cycle
---
The grace period is the time between the end of your billing cycle and the payment due date (usually 21-25 days). If you pay your FULL statement balance by the due date, you pay ZERO interest on purchases. This is called "riding the grace period."

However, if you carry ANY balance from month to month, you LOSE the grace period entirely. Interest starts accruing from the date of each purchase with no grace period. This is why carrying a balance is so expensive — you're paying interest on new purchases from day 1.

To restore your grace period: pay your FULL statement balance (not just the minimum) for two consecutive billing cycles.

---
id: credit_utilization
tags: credit score, utilization, credit limit, credit card
---
Credit utilization (the percentage of your credit limit you're using) is the second most important factor in your credit score after payment history. Guidelines:
- Below 30%: Generally acceptable
- Below 10%: Optimal for score maximization
- 0%: Can actually be slightly worse than 1-3% (shows inactivity)

Utilization is calculated per-card AND across all cards. A single maxed-out card hurts even if overall utilization is low.

Utilization has NO memory — it only reflects the most recent reported balance. If you pay down your cards before the statement closing date, your reported utilization drops immediately. This makes it one of the fastest ways to boost your credit score (can see improvements in 1-2 months).

# Budgeting Frameworks
---
id: budget_50_30_20
tags: budget, rule, framework, savings, needs, wants
---
The 50/30/20 Rule (Senator Elizabeth Warren, "All Your Worth"):
- 50% of after-tax income → Needs (rent, utilities, minimum debt payments, groceries, insurance, transportation)
- 30% of after-tax income → Wants (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, shopping, hobbies)
- 20% of after-tax income → Savings & Debt Payoff (emergency fund, retirement, extra debt payments, investments)

This is a starting framework. If you have high-interest debt (>10% APR), consider temporarily shifting to 50/20/30 — reducing wants to 20% and pushing 30% toward aggressive debt elimination.

If your rent alone exceeds 30% of income, the framework needs adjustment. Focus on the 20% savings/debt floor as non-negotiable and split the remaining 80% between needs and wants.

---
id: emergency_fund_sizing
tags: emergency fund, savings, safety net, job loss
---
Emergency fund sizing guidelines:
- **Minimum viable**: $1,000 (Dave Ramsey "Baby Step 1") — covers minor emergencies while you focus on debt
- **Standard target**: 3-6 months of essential expenses (not income — just the bare minimum to survive: rent, food, utilities, minimum debt payments, insurance)
- **Conservative target**: 6-12 months — recommended if you're self-employed, in a volatile industry, have dependents, or have a single income household

Where to keep it: High-yield savings account (HYSA). As of 2024-2025, top HYSAs pay 4.5-5.0% APY. Never keep your emergency fund in a checking account earning 0.01%, and never invest it in the stock market (it must be liquid and stable).

Top HYSAs: Marcus by Goldman Sachs, Ally Bank, Capital One 360, Discover, Wealthfront Cash Account.

---
id: debt_avalanche_vs_snowball
tags: debt, payoff, avalanche, snowball, strategy, interest
---
Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball:

**Avalanche (Mathematically Optimal)**:
Pay minimums on everything, throw all extra money at the highest APR debt first. This minimizes total interest paid and is always the cheapest path to debt freedom.

**Snowball (Psychologically Optimal)**:
Pay minimums on everything, throw all extra money at the smallest balance first. You get "quick wins" as small debts are eliminated, providing motivation. This costs more in interest but has higher adherence rates in behavioral studies.

**Hybrid approach**: If your highest APR debt is also relatively small, both strategies align. If your highest APR debt is massive and demoralizing, knock out 1-2 small debts first for momentum, then switch to avalanche.

The mathematical difference between strategies is usually $200-$2,000 over the payoff period. The difference between sticking with ANY strategy vs. giving up is tens of thousands of dollars. Pick whichever keeps you motivated.

# Insurance & Negotiation
---
id: insurance_negotiation
tags: insurance, car insurance, negotiation, premium, savings
---
How to lower your insurance premiums:
1. **Shop around annually**: Insurance companies count on inertia. Get quotes from 3-5 competitors every renewal. Average savings from switching: $300-$700/year on auto insurance alone.
2. **Bundle**: Combining auto + renters/home insurance with one carrier typically saves 10-25%.
3. **Raise your deductible**: Going from $500 to $1,000 deductible can reduce premiums by 15-30%. Only do this if you have the emergency fund to cover the higher deductible.
4. **Ask for discounts**: Low mileage (<7,500 mi/year), good student, safe driver, autopay, paperless billing, defensive driving course, professional organization membership. Most carriers don't volunteer these — you must ask.
5. **Review coverage**: If your car is worth less than $5,000, consider dropping comprehensive/collision. The premiums may exceed what you'd receive in a claim.
6. **Credit score matters**: In most states, insurers use credit-based insurance scores. Improving your credit score can lower premiums.

---
id: negotiate_medical_bills
tags: medical, hospital, bill, negotiate, healthcare, debt
---
How to negotiate medical bills:
1. **Always request an itemized bill**: Hospitals frequently include errors or inflated charges. An itemized bill lets you dispute specific line items.
2. **Compare with fair pricing**: Use fairhealthconsumer.org or healthcarebluebook.com to check the fair market price for each procedure code (CPT code) in your zip code.
3. **Ask for the cash/self-pay rate**: Hospitals charge insured patients 2-5x what they charge cash patients. Ask: "What is your self-pay or prompt-pay discount?" This can reduce bills by 30-60%.
4. **Negotiate a payment plan**: Hospitals almost always offer interest-free payment plans. NEVER put medical debt on a credit card (you'd convert 0% debt into 24% debt).
5. **Apply for financial assistance**: Non-profit hospitals are legally required to offer charity care. Ask for their "Financial Assistance Application" — if your income is below 200-400% of the federal poverty level, you may qualify for a full write-off.
6. **Medical debt and credit**: As of 2023, paid medical debt no longer appears on credit reports. Unpaid medical debt under $500 also no longer appears. Medical debt over $500 only appears after 12 months.

# Subscription Optimization
---
id: subscription_audit
tags: subscription, recurring, cancel, save, streaming, optimization
---
Average American spends $219/month on subscriptions (2024 data), but estimates they spend only $86. Common hidden subscriptions:
- Streaming services you haven't used in 30+ days
- Free trials that auto-converted to paid
- App subscriptions buried in App Store/Google Play
- Annual renewals you forgot about (antivirus, domains, cloud storage)
- Gym memberships (average usage drops 80% after February)

Optimization strategies:
1. **Rotate streaming**: Subscribe to one service at a time, binge what you want, cancel, move to next. Most allow re-subscribing instantly.
2. **Family plans**: Spotify Family ($16.99) vs 2 individual accounts ($23.98). Apple One Family bundles iCloud, Music, TV+, Arcade.
3. **Student/military discounts**: Spotify, Amazon Prime, Apple Music, YouTube Premium all offer 50% student discounts. Many extend 4+ years without re-verification.
4. **Annual vs monthly**: If you're keeping a subscription, annual billing is typically 15-35% cheaper. Only commit if you've used the service actively for 3+ months.
5. **Negotiate retention**: Before cancelling, go through the cancellation flow. Many services (Sirius XM, cable, gyms) will offer 30-50% retention discounts to keep you.
