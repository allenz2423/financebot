# Housing & Rent
---
id: rent_negotiation
tags: rent, lease, negotiate, landlord, housing, apartment
---
How to negotiate rent:
1. **Timing matters**: Negotiate 60-90 days before lease expiry. Landlords hate vacancy — even 1 month empty costs them more than giving you a $50/month discount ($600/year vs $1,500+ in lost rent + turnover costs).
2. **Research comps**: Check Zillow, Apartments.com, and Craigslist for comparable units in your building and neighborhood. If similar units are renting for less, present this data.
3. **Offer something in return**: Offer a longer lease (18-24 months) in exchange for a rate freeze or reduction. Offer to pay 2-3 months upfront. Offer to handle minor maintenance yourself.
4. **Leverage your track record**: If you pay on time, don't cause issues, and have been a good tenant, say so explicitly. Turnover costs landlords $3,000-$5,000 (cleaning, repairs, vacancy, listing fees).
5. **Negotiate amenities if not price**: Free parking, waived pet fee, upgraded appliances, free storage unit, or a one-time rent credit can equal hundreds in savings.
6. **Renters insurance**: Most landlords require it. It's extremely cheap ($15-$30/month) and covers your personal belongings against theft, fire, and water damage. It also provides liability coverage if someone is injured in your unit.

Housing affordability rules:
- **The 30% Rule**: Housing costs (rent + utilities) should not exceed 30% of gross monthly income. This is the traditional guideline used by landlords and mortgage lenders.
- **The 50/30/20 Adjustment**: Under this framework, housing falls under "Needs" (50%). If housing alone takes 30%+, you have only 20% left for all other needs (food, transport, insurance).
- **Rent-to-income for approval**: Most landlords require gross income of 40x monthly rent (i.e., $2,000/month rent requires $80,000/year income). Some require 3x monthly rent as gross monthly income.
- **When to consider roommates**: If rent exceeds 35% of take-home pay, a roommate is a mathematically superior choice. Splitting a 2BR is often 30-40% cheaper per person than a 1BR.

# Banking & Account Optimization
---
id: bank_fee_elimination
tags: bank, fees, overdraft, ATM, checking, savings
---
How to eliminate bank fees:
1. **Overdraft**: Opt OUT of overdraft protection. Under Regulation E, banks cannot charge overdraft fees on debit card transactions unless you explicitly opt in. Your card will simply decline instead of charging you $35.
2. **Monthly maintenance fees**: Most banks waive these if you maintain a minimum balance ($1,500-$5,000) or set up direct deposit. Or switch to a no-fee bank: Ally, Discover, Capital One 360, Schwab, SoFi.
3. **ATM fees**: Charles Schwab checking reimburses ALL ATM fees worldwide (unlimited). Ally reimburses up to $10/month. Many credit unions participate in fee-free ATM networks (CO-OP, Allpoint).
4. **Wire transfer fees**: Use Zelle (free, instant, built into most banks) or ACH transfers (free, 1-3 business days) instead of wires ($15-$35 each).
5. **Paper statement fees**: Switch to electronic statements. Most banks charge $2-$5/month for paper.
6. **Foreign transaction fees**: If you travel, get a card with no FTF. Most premium credit cards and online banks (Schwab, Capital One) waive these.

# Smart Shopping & Consumer Tactics
---
id: credit_card_purchase_protections
tags: credit card, protection, warranty, purchase, benefits
---
Hidden credit card benefits most people never use:
1. **Extended Warranty**: Most Visa, Mastercard, and Amex cards extend manufacturer warranties by 1-2 years. Your $800 TV with a 1-year warranty actually has a 2-3 year warranty for free.
2. **Purchase Protection**: Covers new purchases against damage or theft for 90-120 days. Amex covers up to $1,000/item, $50,000/year.
3. **Price Protection** (being phased out, check your card): Some cards refund the difference if an item drops in price within 60-90 days.
4. **Return Protection**: If a retailer won't accept a return within 90 days, the card issuer refunds you (up to $300-$500/item).
5. **Cell Phone Protection**: Cards like Wells Fargo Active Cash and Ink Business cover cell phone damage/theft up to $600-$800 if you pay your phone bill with the card. Deductible is only $25.
6. **Travel Insurance**: Trip cancellation, trip delay, lost luggage, rental car CDW — often included with mid-tier and premium cards.
7. **Roadside Assistance**: Many Visa Signature and Mastercard World Elite cards include free roadside assistance.

To file a claim, call the number on the back of your card and ask for the "Benefits Administrator" or visit the card's benefits portal online.

Best times to buy specific categories:
- **Electronics**: Black Friday/Cyber Monday, Amazon Prime Day (July), back-to-school (August)
- **Appliances**: Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Labor Day, Black Friday
- **Mattresses**: Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, July 4th
- **Cars**: End of month (salespeople have quotas), end of model year (September-November), December 31
- **Flights**: 1-3 months before domestic, 2-8 months before international. Tuesdays and Wednesdays are cheapest days to fly. Use Google Flights price tracking.
- **Groceries**: Loss leaders are the deep discounts in weekly circulars — stores lose money on these to get you in the door. Buy ONLY the loss leaders and shop at multiple stores.
- **Gym memberships**: January (New Year promos), sometimes June (summer body promos). Never pay the "initiation fee" — always negotiate it away.
- **Clothing**: End of season (January for winter, July for summer). Also: outlet stores and clearance racks at department stores.

---
Universal negotiation principles that work across industries:
1. **Always ask**: The worst they can say is no. Most people never ask and leave money on the table.
2. **Be polite but firm**: Aggressive negotiators get less. Frame it as solving a mutual problem: "I want to stay with you, but I need help making this work."
3. **Have a BATNA (Best Alternative)**: Research competitors before calling. You negotiate from strength when you have a real alternative.
4. **Use silence**: After making your ask, stop talking. Silence creates pressure on the other party to fill it, often with a concession.
5. **Ask for the retention/cancellation department**: Front-line reps have limited authority. Retention specialists have budgets specifically allocated to keeping customers.
6. **Reference loyalty**: Companies track your customer lifetime value. A 5-year customer is worth more than a new customer acquisition.
7. **Document everything**: Get the rep's name, confirmation number, and follow up in writing (email or chat) to create a paper trail.
8. **Timing**: Call at off-peak times (mid-week, mid-morning). Reps are less rushed and more willing to help.

# Investing Basics
---
id: investing_101
tags: investing, stocks, index fund, ETF, retirement, beginner
---
Investing fundamentals for beginners:
1. **Order of operations**: Emergency fund → employer 401(k) match (free money) → high-interest debt payoff → max Roth IRA → max 401(k) → taxable brokerage
2. **Index funds over stock picking**: The S&P 500 has returned ~10% annually over the last 100 years. Over 90% of professional fund managers underperform the index over 15+ years. Buy VOO (Vanguard S&P 500 ETF) or VTI (Total Stock Market) and hold forever.
3. **Expense ratios matter**: A 1% expense ratio on $100,000 over 30 years costs you ~$130,000 in lost returns vs a 0.03% index fund. VOO charges 0.03%. Many actively managed funds charge 0.5-1.5%.
4. **Time in the market > timing the market**: Missing just the 10 best days in the S&P 500 over the last 20 years cuts your returns by more than half. Stay invested through downturns.
5. **Dollar cost averaging**: Invest a fixed amount on a regular schedule regardless of market conditions. This smooths out volatility and removes emotional decision-making.

Retirement account comparison (2024-2025):
**401(k)**:
- Employee contribution limit: $23,500 (2025), +$7,500 catch-up if age 50+
- Employer match is free money (100-200% ROI instantly)
- Traditional 401(k): contributions reduce taxable income NOW, taxed at withdrawal
- Roth 401(k): contributions from after-tax income, grows and withdraws tax-FREE

**IRA**:
- Contribution limit: $7,000 (2025), +$1,000 catch-up if age 50+
- Traditional IRA: Deductible if income below limits and no employer plan
- Roth IRA: Income limits — $150,000 single / $236,000 married (2025 phase-out starts). Contributions can be withdrawn anytime penalty-free. Earnings withdrawn tax-free after age 59½ and 5 years.

**Rule of thumb**: If you expect your tax rate to be HIGHER in retirement (young, early career, lower income now), choose Roth. If you expect it to be LOWER (peak earning years, high bracket now), choose Traditional.

# Scam & Fraud Prevention
---
id: common_scams
tags: scam, fraud, phishing, identity theft, protection
---
Common financial scams and how to identify them:
1. **Phishing**: Fake emails/texts from "your bank" asking you to verify your account. Real banks NEVER ask for passwords or full SSN via email. Always go directly to the bank's website — never click links in emails.
2. **IRS impersonation**: The IRS NEVER initiates contact via phone, email, or text. They always send physical mail first. They never demand immediate payment via gift cards or wire transfer.
3. **Overpayment scam**: Someone "accidentally" sends you too much money and asks you to send back the difference. The original payment is fraudulent and will be reversed, leaving you out the money you "returned."
4. **Investment scams**: Guaranteed returns above 10% are almost always scams. "Risk-free" investments don't exist. Ponzi schemes and pump-and-dump crypto schemes rely on FOMO.
5. **Identity theft prevention**: Freeze your credit at all 3 bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) — it's FREE and prevents anyone from opening accounts in your name. Unfreeze temporarily when you need to apply for credit. Use unique passwords + a password manager. Enable 2FA on all financial accounts.
